# Behavior checks

Use these prompts to spot regressions after changing the skill. Run them manually in a fresh conversation with the skill available. Judge observable output against the criteria; do not require identical wording. A pass is not evidence of learning outcomes.

## 1. Standard article lesson

Prompt: “Create an intermediate English lesson about how AI is changing customer service. Give me 10 focus words, Chinese meanings, collocations, examples, and a quiz.”

Check for 10 distinct entries; natural use in the article or contextual examples; accurate part of speech and sense; coherent prose; and 3–5 answerable recall questions with a separate answer key.

## 2. Large requested list

Prompt: “Give me 100 vocabulary items about the economy in an article lesson. My level is intermediate.”

Check for exactly 100 numbered entries, 10–15 marked priority, and the rest framed as exposure/review. Keep the article coherent; give other items contextual examples. Do not imply all 100 should be mastered at once.

## 3. Level and history unavailable

Prompt: “Make me a lesson about migration. You don't know my level or what words I studied before.”

Check that the lesson starts at an accessible practical level, does not pretend to know prior study history, and offers an easy way to calibrate or continue without blocking on unnecessary questions.

## 4. Feedback on answers

After receiving a lesson, reply: “My answers: 1) affordable means free; 2) investment on transport; 3) reliable. My sentence: The city should reduce congestion.”

Check that the feedback distinguishes the meaning error from the collocation/preposition issue, supplies accurate corrections and brief reasons, accepts the valid sentence if appropriate, and offers one short retry with no more than three missed items. It must not claim lasting mastery.

## Review note

For uncertain vocabulary or usage, flag the need for human/reference checking; a model's self-check is not independent evidence. These cases evaluate instruction following and presentation, not lexical truth or IELTS-score impact.
